Are we measuring the right emissions?

25 March 2026First posted on LinkedIn

In sustainability, we celebrate hard. A 5% reduction in total emissions? That's months of work, stakeholder meetings, tool deployments and factory-level data collection. And honestly? It should be celebrated.

But here's a thought I can't shake. While we're engineering precision GHG tracking tools to squeeze every gram of CO₂ out of a factory floor, has anyone looked up the carbon cost of a single airstrike?

  • One cruise missile: about 1.5 tons of CO₂
  • One fighter jet sortie: about 3–8 tons of CO₂
  • Gaza bombardment, first month alone: an estimated 281,000 tons of CO₂

That's the equivalent of burning 300 million pounds of coal, or the annual emissions of 60,000 cars. All in 30 days.

And yet no sustainability report covers it. No ESG framework flags it. No net-zero roadmap even mentions it. We build tools to track Scope 1, 2 and 3 emissions down to the gram. Defence budgets don't ask about fuel efficiency. Ever.

Are we solving the problem, or just managing what's measurable? If the goal is genuinely “same planet for everyone,” shouldn't all emissions be on the table?

P.S. No sustainability strategy has ever included a section on wartime emissions. Maybe that silence is worth questioning.
