Why 1900 wasn't a leap year, but 2000 was

Most of us learned that leap years happen every four years. That's only part of the rule.

A year must be divisible by 4, but century years (like 1900, 2000, 2100) must also be divisible by 400.

  • 1900 ÷ 400 = 4.75, so not a leap year
  • 2000 ÷ 400 = 5, so a leap year

That extra condition keeps our calendar aligned with Earth's orbit over centuries. Without it, the seasons would slowly drift.

Small rule. Big long-term impact. Sometimes the details matter more than the headline.
